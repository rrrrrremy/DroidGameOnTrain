Droid

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 August 2026

Droid is a word game published by Second Nature Games Pty Limited (ACN 161 671 549), an Australian company. This policy covers the Droid iOS app and the website at droidgame.web.app.

The short version. There are no accounts and no sign-in. We do not use analytics, advertising, or tracking of any kind, and we do not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, location, or any device identifier. The only information that ever leaves your device is a leaderboard entry — and only if you choose to submit one.

What we collect

Leaderboard entries (optional). After a daily game you may choose to post your result to the public leaderboard. If you do, we store:

  • two initials you choose (letters only, e.g. “AB”);
  • your score and the maximum score for that puzzle;
  • your score as a percentage;
  • the date of the puzzle and which board shape you played;
  • the time the entry was received.

That is the complete list. The name field accepts two letters and nothing else — not a full name, not free text — so a leaderboard entry cannot carry personal information even if someone tried to put it there. Nothing links an entry back to you, your device, or your other entries. If you never post a score, we store nothing about you at all.

Information stored only on your device. The game keeps a small amount of state in your device's local storage: which puzzle you played today, a cached copy of the current puzzle, and a short list of recently used words so puzzles do not repeat. This never leaves your device and is erased if you delete the app or clear your browser data.

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, postal address, date of birth, contacts, photos, calendar, microphone or camera input, health data, precise or coarse location, or any advertising identifier. Droid does not ask for tracking permission because it does not track you.

Third-party services

Google Firebase (Google LLC) hosts the website and stores the leaderboard. In the normal course of serving a request, Google receives technical information such as your IP address — standard for any internet service. Google's handling of that data is covered by the Firebase privacy documentation and Google's privacy policy. We do not enable Google Analytics or any Firebase analytics product.

Word validation. The game's dictionary is bundled with the app. Words are checked on your device, including when you are offline; no words are sent to a dictionary service.

Lightning payments (website only). On the website, an optional extra game can be unlocked with a Bitcoin Lightning payment. Payments are receive-only, settle to a node we operate, and carry no personal information. We are not sent and do not store any wallet identity, card number, or billing detail. This feature is not present in the iOS app.

There are no advertising networks, no analytics SDKs, no social-media SDKs, and no other third-party code in the app.

How the information is used

Leaderboard entries are used for one thing: to display the public daily leaderboard inside the game. We do not profile players, build audience segments, send marketing, or make automated decisions about anyone. We do not sell, rent, or share personal information with third parties, and we never have.

How long it is kept

Leaderboard entries are kept so historical daily results remain viewable. Because entries carry no account or device identifier, we generally cannot tell which entry belongs to which person — so if you want one removed, email us with the initials, the date, and the score, and we will delete any entry we can identify from that. Data held only on your device is removed when you delete the app or clear your browser data.

Your rights

Under the Australian Privacy Principles — and comparable laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA where they apply to you — you may ask us what information we hold about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, and complain about how we have handled it. Contact us at the address below and we will respond within a reasonable time. Australian residents may also complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

Children

Droid is suitable for a general audience but is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The only field a player can type into accepts two letters, which cannot hold personal information.

Security

All traffic between the game and our services uses HTTPS. The leaderboard database accepts new entries only, in a fixed and validated format — existing entries cannot be edited or deleted by players — and no other data is stored server-side.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes we will update the page and the "last updated" date above. Material changes will be reflected here before they take effect.
